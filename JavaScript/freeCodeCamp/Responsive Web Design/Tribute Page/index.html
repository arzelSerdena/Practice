Tim Berners-Lee

The man who created the internet.

Here's a time line of Tim Berners-Lee life:

  • 1955 - Born in London, England, to computer scientist parents who worked on one of the earliest computers, the Ferranti Mark 1.
  • 1976 - Graduates from Queen's College, Oxford, with a degree in physics. During his time at Oxford, builds his first computer using a soldering iron, an old television, and spare parts.
  • 1980 - Begins working as a software engineer at CERN, the European Organization for Nuclear Research. Creates "Enquire," a software program designed to store and link information between different documents—an early conceptual model of the World Wide Web.
  • 1989 - Proposes the idea of a global hypertext system at CERN. The proposal outlines what would later become the World Wide Web, offering a way to share information between computers via the internet. He envisions a decentralized platform where anyone could contribute content.
  • 1990 - Develops the first-ever web server (called "httpd") and the first web browser, and publishes the world’s first website (info.cern.ch). This website explains how the World Wide Web works and how people can set up their own servers and create websites.
  • 1991 - The first web page becomes accessible to the public, marking the birth of the World Wide Web.
  • 1993 - The World Wide Web is released to the public domain, allowing anyone to use it for free. This leads to the rapid growth of websites and internet users worldwide.
  • 1994 - Founds the World Wide Web Consortium (W3C) at MIT to create international standards for the web and to ensure it remains open and accessible to everyone.
  • 2004 - Knighted by Queen Elizabeth II for his services to the global development of the internet. He receives numerous other prestigious awards for his contributions, including the Millennium Technology Prize.
  • 2009 - Appointed to the Order of Merit, a highly distinguished honor in the UK, in recognition of his pioneering work on the World Wide Web.
  • 2012 - Actively campaigns for internet freedom, net neutrality, and privacy. Berners-Lee often emphasizes the importance of keeping the web a free, open platform for innovation and expression.
  • 2014 - Celebrates the 25th anniversary of the World Wide Web. Berners-Lee continues to advocate for internet access as a basic human right and speaks against the growing threats to privacy and data security.
  • 2020 - Co-founds the "Contract for the Web," a global plan to make the internet safer and more accessible, protect people's privacy, and prevent the web from being weaponized for harm.
  • 2023 - Announces the Solid Project, a new initiative aimed at giving individuals more control over their own data, combating issues of data privacy, ownership, and misuse.
"There was a time when people felt the internet was another world, but now people realise it's a tool that we use in this world."
-Tim Berners-Lee
